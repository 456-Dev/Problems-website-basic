The premise

Michael walks up to strangers, asks every one of them the same question, and keeps nearly every answer. Two hundred episodes of it — from New York to Fiji, Istanbul, Dakar and Rio. See the map.

No question has a right answer. The fun is in how many different directions twenty-five people can take the same few words.

The dial

The dial puts every question on one face. Around it: the topic. Outward: the mood, heavy near the middle, silly at the rim. The colour says what the question asks of you — a big question, a hot take, or something about you.

It opens on the Question of the Day — a new one every day. Point at (or tap) any mark to read another; left alone, it wanders through them by itself.

How the tags work

Read, not measured

Topic, mood and kind of question are editorial calls, made by reading each question — never by analysing anyone's answers. Disagree with one? Tell Michael on Instagram, @bignosemichael.

Your turn

Every episode page asks you the question too. Written answers go up straight away, unfiltered, like the strangers’. Filmed ones are watched first. Then the page asks you the next question.
